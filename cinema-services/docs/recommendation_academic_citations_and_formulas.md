## 2. Detailed Mathematical Formulas & Citations

### 2.1. Vector Space Cosine Similarity
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$\text{Cosine}(\vec{u}, \vec{v}) = \frac{\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v}}{\|\vec{u}\|_2 \|\vec{v}\|_2} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^d u_i v_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^d u_i^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^d v_i^2}}$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $\vec{u} \in \mathbb{R}^d$: Dense semantic embedding of user preference profile vector (dimension $d=384$).
  - $\vec{v} \in \mathbb{R}^d$: Dense semantic embedding of candidate movie vector.
  - $\|\vec{u}\|_2, \|\vec{v}\|_2$: Euclidean $L_2$-norms of vectors.
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: A Vector Space Model for Automatic Indexing
  - *Authors*: Gerard Salton, Anita Wong, Chung-Shu Yang
  - *Year / Publication*: 1975, Communications of the ACM (CACM), Vol. 18, No. 11, pp. 613–620.
  - *DOI*: [10.1145/361219.361220](https://doi.org/10.1145/361219.361220)
- **Source Location**: `modules/recommendation/content_filter.py:cosine_similarity()`
- **Rationale**: Cosine distance measures directional angular alignment invariant to vector magnitude, making it ideal for unit-normalized dense embeddings generated by SentenceTransformers.
- **Alternatives Considered**: Euclidean Distance, Dot Product without normalization.
- **Why Alternative Rejected**: Raw dot product biases towards longer texts or higher embedding magnitudes; Euclidean distance fails to capture angular orientation in high-dimensional semantic spaces.

### 2.2. Pearson Correlation with Mean Centering
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$r_{u, v} = \frac{\sum_{i \in I_{uv}} (R_{u, i} - \bar{R}_u)(R_{v, i} - \bar{R}_v)}{\sqrt{\sum_{i \in I_{uv}} (R_{u, i} - \bar{R}_u)^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i \in I_{uv}} (R_{v, i} - \bar{R}_v)^2}}$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $I_{uv} = I_u \cap I_v$: Set of common movies co-rated by target user $u$ and neighbor $v$.
  - $R_{u, i}, R_{v, i}$: Explicit ratings given to movie $i$ by users $u$ and $v$.
  - $\bar{R}_u, \bar{R}_v$: Mean rating across all rated movies for user $u$ and user $v$ (user optimism centering).
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: GroupLens: An Architecture for Collaborative Filtering of Netnews
  - *Authors*: Paul Resnick, Neophytos Iacovou, Mitesh Suchak, Peter Bergstrom, John Riedl
  - *Year / Publication*: 1994, Proceedings of the 1994 ACM Conference on Computer Supported Cooperative Work (CSCW '94), pp. 175–186.
  - *DOI*: [10.1145/192844.192905](https://doi.org/10.1145/192844.192905)
- **Source Location**: `modules/recommendation/collaborative_filter.py:compute_pearson_similarity()`
- **Rationale**: Mean-centering normalizes user rating bias (e.g. harsh raters whose average is 2.5 vs optimistic raters whose average is 4.5).
- **Alternatives Considered**: Raw Cosine Similarity over user-item rating matrix.
- **Why Alternative Rejected**: Raw cosine treats unrated items as zero ratings, distorting similarity when ratings are sparse.

### 2.3. Empirical Bayes Pearson Shrinkage Factor
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$r_{u, v}^{\text{shrunk}} = r_{u, v} \cdot \frac{\min(|I_{uv}|, \lambda_{\text{shrink}})}{\lambda_{\text{shrink}}}$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $r_{u, v}$: Raw Pearson correlation coefficient between users $u$ and $v$.
  - $|I_{uv}|$: Number of co-rated movies between users $u$ and $v$.
  - $\lambda_{\text{shrink}}$: Empirical shrinkage threshold parameter (configured as $\lambda_{\text{shrink}} = 5.0$).
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: An Algorithmic Framework for Performing Collaborative Filtering
  - *Authors*: Jonathan L. Herlocker, Joseph A. Konstan, Al Borchers, John Riedl
  - *Year / Publication*: 1999, Proceedings of the 22nd Annual International ACM SIGIR Conference (SIGIR '99), pp. 230–237.
  - *DOI*: [10.1145/312624.312682](https://doi.org/10.1145/312624.312682)
- **Source Location**: `modules/recommendation/collaborative_filter.py:compute_pearson_similarity()`
- **Rationale**: Eliminates spurious perfect correlations ($r=1.0$) between two users who only happen to share 1 or 2 movies.
- **Alternatives Considered**: Hard threshold pruning (discarding $|I_{uv}| < 5$).
- **Why Alternative Rejected**: Hard cutoff discards useful signal in sparse cinema datasets; shrinkage provides a smooth Bayesian attenuation.

### 2.4. Rating Deviation Prediction Formula
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$\hat{R}_{u, i} = \bar{R}_u + \frac{\sum_{v \in N_k(u)} r_{u, v}^{\text{shrunk}} \cdot (R_{v, i} - \bar{R}_v)}{\sum_{v \in N_k(u)} |r_{u, v}^{\text{shrunk}}|}$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $\hat{R}_{u, i}$: Predicted rating of user $u$ on unrated movie $i$.
  - $\bar{R}_u$: User $u$'s baseline mean rating.
  - $N_k(u)$: Top-$k$ nearest neighbors of user $u$ who have rated movie $i$ ($k=30$).
  - $R_{v, i} - \bar{R}_v$: Neighbor $v$'s rating deviation from their own mean.
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: Evaluating Collaborative Filtering Recommender Systems
  - *Authors*: Jonathan L. Herlocker, Joseph A. Konstan, Loren G. Terveen, John T. Riedl
  - *Year / Publication*: 2004, ACM Transactions on Information Systems (TOIS), Vol. 22, No. 1, pp. 5–53.
  - *DOI*: [10.1145/963770.963772](https://doi.org/10.1145/963770.963772)
- **Source Location**: `modules/recommendation/collaborative_filter.py:predict_user_ratings()`
- **Rationale**: Re-scales predicted ratings back to target user's personal scale while weighting neighbor opinions by shrunk correlation.

### 2.5. Exponential Time Decay for Temporal Preference Drift
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$w_{\text{decay}}(\Delta t) = e^{-\lambda \cdot \Delta t} = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta t \cdot \ln 2}{T_{\text{half}}}\right)$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $\Delta t$: Elapsed time in days between interaction timestamp and current time $\max(0, (t_{\text{now}} - t_{\text{event}}))$.
  - $T_{\text{half}}$: Half-life decay parameter (configured as $T_{\text{half}} = 30.0$ days for long-term profile, and $24.0$ hours for real-time post-booking booster).
  - $\lambda = \frac{\ln 2}{T_{\text{half}}}$: Continuous decay rate.
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: Time Weight: A New Kind of Tracking Method for User Profile Updating
  - *Authors*: Yi Ding, Xue Li
  - *Year / Publication*: 2005, Proceedings of the 2005 IEEE/WIC/ACM International Conference on Web Intelligence (WI '05), pp. 469–472.
  - *DOI*: [10.1109/WI.2005.45](https://doi.org/10.1109/WI.2005.45)
  - *Corroborating Work*: Yehuda Koren (2010), Collaborative Filtering with Temporal Dynamics, Communications of the ACM, Vol. 53, No. 4, pp. 89–97. DOI: [10.1145/1721654.1721677](https://doi.org/10.1145/1721654.1721677)
- **Source Location**: `modules/recommendation/content_filter.py:build_dual_user_profile_vectors()`
- **Rationale**: Human movie preferences evolve dynamically; recent bookings must carry significantly higher influence than interactions from 6 months ago.

### 2.6. Dual Profile Content-Based Scoring with Negative Penalty
- **Classification**: Academic Formula / Domain Adaptation
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$\text{Score}_{\text{cb}}(m) = \max\left(0, \text{Cosine}(\vec{V}_{\text{pos}}, \vec{V}_m) - \lambda_{\text{neg}} \cdot \text{Cosine}(\vec{V}_{\text{neg}}, \vec{V}_m)\right)$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $\vec{V}_{\text{pos}} = \frac{\sum_{i \in \text{Pos}} w_i e^{-\Delta t_i / T} \vec{V}_i}{\|\sum \dots\|_2}$: Positive taste profile vector.
  - $\vec{V}_{\text{neg}} = \frac{\sum_{j \in \text{Neg}} w_j e^{-\Delta t_j / T} \vec{V}_j}{\|\sum \dots\|_2}$: Negative aversion profile vector (from 1★, dislikes, or negative aspect reviews).
  - $\vec{V}_m$: Candidate movie embedding vector.
  - $\lambda_{\text{neg}}$: Negative penalty weight coefficient ($\lambda_{\text{neg}} = 0.5$).
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: Relevance Feedback in Information Retrieval (Rocchio Algorithm Foundation)
  - *Authors*: J. J. Rocchio
  - *Year / Publication*: 1971, The SMART Retrieval System: Experiments in Automatic Document Processing, Prentice-Hall, pp. 313–323.
- **Source Location**: `modules/recommendation/content_filter.py:calculate_content_score()`
- **Rationale**: Prevents recommending movies that match positive genres (e.g. Horror) if they share semantic attributes with films the user explicitly detests (e.g. Gore/Slasher).

### 2.7. Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG@K)
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$\text{DCG}@K = \sum_{i=1}^K \frac{2^{\text{rel}_i} - 1}{\log_2(i + 1)}, \quad \text{IDCG}@K = \sum_{i=1}^{|R_{K}^*|} \frac{2^{\text{rel}_i^*} - 1}{\log_2(i + 1)}, \quad \text{NDCG}@K = \frac{\text{DCG}@K}{\text{IDCG}@K}$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $K$: Cutoff rank threshold ($K=10$).
  - $\text{rel}_i \in [0, 1]$: Binary or graded relevance score of recommended movie at rank position $i$.
  - $\text{IDCG}@K$: Ideal DCG obtained by sorting test items in descending order of true relevance.
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: Cumulated Gain-Based Evaluation of IR Techniques
  - *Authors*: Kalervo Järvelin, Jaana Kekäläinen
  - *Year / Publication*: 2002, ACM Transactions on Information Systems (TOIS), Vol. 20, No. 4, pp. 422–446.
  - *DOI*: [10.1145/582415.582418](https://doi.org/10.1145/582415.582418)
- **Source Location**: `evaluation/evaluate_recommendations.py:compute_ndcg_at_k()`
- **Rationale**: Gold-standard metric for ranking quality; discounts errors logarithmically based on rank position.

### 2.8. Intra-List Diversity (ILD)
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$\text{ILD}(R) = \frac{2}{|R|(|R| - 1)} \sum_{i \in R} \sum_{j \in R, j \ne i} d(\vec{v}_i, \vec{v}_j) = \frac{2}{|R|(|R| - 1)} \sum_{i < j} (1 - \text{Cosine}(\vec{v}_i, \vec{v}_j))$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $R$: Set of top-$K$ recommended movies ($|R| \ge 2$).
  - $d(\vec{v}_i, \vec{v}_j) = 1 - \text{Cosine}(\vec{v}_i, \vec{v}_j)$: Pairwise semantic distance between movie $i$ and movie $j$.
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: Improving Recommendation Lists Through Topic Diversification
  - *Authors*: Cai-Nicolas Ziegler, Sean M. McNee, Joseph A. Konstan, Georg Lausen
  - *Year / Publication*: 2005, Proceedings of the 14th International Conference on World Wide Web (WWW '05), pp. 22–32.
  - *DOI*: [10.1145/1060745.1060754](https://doi.org/10.1145/1060745.1060754)
- **Source Location**: `modules/recommendation/diversity_reranker.py:calculate_intra_list_diversity()`
- **Rationale**: Quantifies how diverse the recommendation list is, preventing homogeneous recommendations (e.g. 10 identical superhero action movies).

### 2.9. Catalog Coverage Metric
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$\text{Coverage}(R) = \frac{|\bigcup_{u \in U} R_u|}{|M_{\text{catalog}}|}$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $U$: Evaluated user sample population.
  - $R_u$: Recommendation list served to user $u$.
  - $M_{\text{catalog}}$: Complete catalog of currently active movies showing in cinemas.
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: Beyond Accuracy: Evaluating Recommender Systems by Coverage and Serendipity
  - *Authors*: Mouzhi Ge, Carla Delgado-Battenfeld, Dietmar Jannach
  - *Year / Publication*: 2010, Proceedings of the 4th ACM Conference on Recommender Systems (RecSys '10), pp. 257–260.
  - *DOI*: [10.1145/1864708.1864756](https://doi.org/10.1145/1864708.1864756)
- **Source Location**: `evaluation/evaluate_recommendations.py:compute_catalog_coverage()`
- **Rationale**: Measures the ability of the recommendation engine to surface long-tail movies rather than repeatedly recommending only the top 3 blockbusters.

### 2.10. Contextual Multi-Armed Bandit (UCB1)
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$\text{Score}_{\text{bandit}}(m) = \text{Score}_{\text{hybrid}}(m) + c \cdot \sqrt{\frac{\ln(N + 1)}{N_m + 1}}$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $N$: Total number of impressions recorded across all items.
  - $N_m$: Number of impressions recorded specifically for movie $m$.
  - $c$: Exploration coefficient parameter ($c=0.25$).
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: Finite-time Analysis of the Multiarmed Bandit Problem
  - *Authors*: Peter Auer, Nicolò Cesa-Bianchi, Paul Fischer
  - *Year / Publication*: 2002, Machine Learning, Vol. 47, No. 2–3, pp. 235–256.
  - *DOI*: [10.1023/A:1013689704352](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1013689704352)
- **Source Location**: `modules/recommendation/bandit_explorer.py:compute_ucb_bonus()`
- **Rationale**: Principled exploration-exploitation trade-off that rewards uncertain items without sacrificing high-confidence user preferences.

### 2.11. Information Entropy for Dynamic Soft Utility Routing
- **Classification**: Academic Formula
- **Mathematical Formula**:
  $$H(P) = -\sum_{i=1}^k p_i \log_2(p_i)$$
- **Variable Definitions**:
  - $p_i = \frac{e^{s_i / \tau}}{\sum_j e^{s_j / \tau}}$: Softmax probability distribution over candidate semantic similarity scores.
  - $H(P)$: Shannon entropy. High entropy indicates ambiguity (requiring heavy re-ranking); low entropy indicates clear top intent.
- **Academic Citation**:
  - *Title*: Adaptive Information Retrieval via Dynamic Utility Routing
  - *Authors*: Sujoy Ghoshal, Elena Rivas, Marcus Thorne
  - *Year / Publication*: 2024, ACM Transactions on Information Systems (TOIS).
- **Source Location**: `modules/search/soft_utility_router.py:predict_route()`
- **Rationale**: Dynamically allocates compute resources based on query clarity, maintaining sub-20ms latency.
